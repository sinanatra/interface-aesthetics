# Case Study
## How do we design interfaces that last?
![](https://d2w9rnfcy7mm78.cloudfront.net/52829205/original_38caecd905aa18466f54e8a6b2183913.png?1794836855?bc=0)


In Bologna, next to therapy, there are many activities that can help someone in a fragile moment, from sport to cultural programmes. In mental health they are called social psychiatry, and sometimes they are prescribed as part of a path of care. They are usually suggested by a professional, and each professional knows only part of them. Two people with the same need can be sent in very different directions, depending on the service and the person they happen to meet.

## Scattered

The information exists, but it is scattered. The city has a [map of community resources](https://sitmappe.comune.bologna.it/RisorseSociali/). Services draw on other lists or make their own, and almost always they are built by those who provide care, not by those who look for it. [Pirate Care](https://pirate.care/) insists on horizontal knowledge, what people learn from each other. None of these tools collects it.
Some interfaces depend on one person, one grant, or a digital service that can close overnight. Since 2001 Autistici/Inventati hosted email and websites for Italian movements and associations. In August 2026 the United States put it on a sanctions list, and in September the collective [suspended every service](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2026/09/07/autistici-inventati-sanzioni-trump-chiusura-notizie/8498485/). The ones that last are often the simplest: a phone number, a sheet pinned in a waiting room, people who know each other.

## Needs first

Before designing, we start from needs. Who looks for these activities, and who suggests, knows or runs them? Only then do we ask what shape the answer should take: with which names and categories, in which language, on which medium. Whatever we propose has to be understood, used, copied and maintained by others, without depending on whoever designed it.
