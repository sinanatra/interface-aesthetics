# Gardens
## Can a website be inhabited?

> My website is a shifting house next to a river of knowledge.
> Laurel Schwulst, ["My Website Is a Shifting House"](https://laurelschwulst.com/e/my-website-is-a-shifting-house/), 2018

A website is usually planned as a project, with a launch date. A garden has none, and is never finished: it grows by tending, and changes with whoever looks after it. In 1995 the Telegarden joined the two, literally: anyone online could plant and water seeds through a robot arm. [Laurel Schwulst](https://laurelschwulst.com/e/my-website-is-a-shifting-house/) asks her students to describe a website as a place where someone lives.

## Homepages

Before platforms, people built their own pages, and the web looked like them. GeoCities grouped homepages into neighbourhoods; when it closed in 2009, the rescued copy could be drawn as [a city](http://deletedcity.net/). Olia Lialina calls this amateur language the [*Vernacular Web*](https://contemporary-home-computing.org/vernacular-web-2/). Her [*Summer*](https://art.teleportacia.org/olia/summer/) is an animation whose every frame lives on someone else's site. It plays only as long as all of them stay online.

## Spreadsheets

Almost everyone can read and write a spreadsheet. Haacke's typed sheets were one too. Rows and columns give a structure; people fill it, annotate it, colour it, and keep it together. Underneath is often a CSV, plain text any program can open (Guillaume Plique wrote it a [love letter](https://medialab.sciencespo.fr/en/news/a-love-letter-to-the-csv-format)). A grid can hold [a drawing](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/17JTNoOGFI0PXPpUVsDCHnq_76B38mv-bc6U64mp6Vt0/edit?usp=sharing), [a publication](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1ulWL11fkAagmib_Bgb2NOEIJhqcmT4U866RjllE4ckA/edit#gid=766678385), [an index](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1ZojSsSn8EnCyU9Knjb8JBYU4vkUKxpyGySq2WzDuNIY/edit?gid=891834841#gid=891834841) of where institutions stand. Lev Manovich called database and narrative natural enemies. In a spreadsheet they share the same cells.

## Coming back

Some places exist only because people keep coming back. [Webrings](https://emreed.net/LowTech_Directory) and the [Corners of the Internet Database](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1TVxcOA-xuxOifbtFv23K-JTJziOEmCEiXtfmZPxK0-E/edit?gid=0#gid=0) link small sites picked by people, not by an algorithm. On [*r/place*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R/place) a shared canvas became territory: groups drew together, and defended what they drew.

<br>

_In short:_ A website can be tended and lived in, like a homepage or a shared spreadsheet.
