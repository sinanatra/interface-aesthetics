Data looks like a description of the world. It is closer to a decision about it. In 1971 Hans Haacke collected public property records on 142 Manhattan buildings owned through one family's companies, and showed them as photographs and typed sheets. Nothing was secret. Making it readable was enough for the Guggenheim to cancel the show.

## Taken, not given

Someone decides what to record, how to name it and how to show it. Johanna Drucker suggests calling data [*capta*](http://www.digitalhumanities.org/dhq/vol/5/1/000091/000091.html), taken rather than given, so that every chart is already a choice. Still, Catherine D'Ignazio notes, we [accept charts as facts](https://civic.mit.edu/index.html%3Fp=2165.html) because they look scientific, general and neutral. Geoffrey Bowker and Susan Leigh Star show the same for classifications ([*Sorting Things Out*](https://attachments.are.na/49371746/f0556d23ff5e875693bb1a2fdcd1f448.pdf)): each one makes some lives visible and others invisible. A diagnosis is one of them. D'Ignazio and Lauren Klein ask anyone working with data three things ([*Data Feminism*](https://attachments.are.na/46622501/5fdd11ff6d2ae5f54b41d42007e57271.pdf)): by whom, for whom, in whose interest?

## Pointing

Charles Sanders Peirce split signs in three. An icon looks like what it shows, a symbol stands for it by convention, an index is connected to it, like smoke to fire or a footprint to a foot. Most charts are symbols: a bar means a number because we agreed it does. Dietmar Offenhuber and Orkan Telhan look at the opposite case, [indexical visualisation](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781315781129-31): displays that don't translate the data but point to the thing itself. Here sensing and sense-making, from the [about](/about) page, come apart. In [*Cloud Studies*](https://forensic-architecture.org/investigation/cloudstudies) (2020) Forensic Architecture reads clouds of tear gas, smoke and toxic fumes as evidence, modelling how each one formed and spread. The cloud registers what happened. Someone still has to make sense of it.

## Evidence

[Forensic Architecture](https://forensic-architecture.org/investigation/beirut-port-explosion) and [Earshot](https://earshot.ngo/investigations/22-february-2023-nablus) rebuild events from videos, photos and sounds that strangers posted online, to show where and how violence happened. Eyal Weizman and Matthew Fuller call this [investigative aesthetics](https://attachments.are.na/43821297/84e2b77272ed574edb3e2889ce6d4cb2.pdf): making something perceptible so that it can be contested.
The result often looks raw on purpose, satellite images and phone videos stitched into one collage, as if rawness made it true. It isn't raw. Someone collected the material, chose it and put it in order, and that is where the design happens.

In 2025 every boat of the Global Sumud Flotilla to Gaza broadcast its position on a public map. Being seen discourages violence, and records it when it happens. With the blue dot you are watched. Here people chose to be.
