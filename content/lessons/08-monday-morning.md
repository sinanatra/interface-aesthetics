# Monday Morning

## Who maintains an interface?

> After the revolution, who’s going to pick up the garbage on Monday morning?
> Mierle Laderman Ukeles, [_Manifesto for Maintenance Art_](https://attachments.are.na/16135948/037f8dd64f656398c2a4b8928ce1b676.pdf), 1969

The work of Mierle Laderman Ukeles places great emphasis on maintenance, which too often goes unnoticed. Digital interfaces are no exception: much of the work happens after launch, and no one puts it on display. [Joan Tronto](https://syllabus.pirate.care/library/Joan%20C.%20Tronto/) and Berenice Fisher define care as everything we do to maintain our world and repair it. Shannon Mattern explores this work in [‘Maintenance and Care’](https://placesjournal.org/article/maintenance-and-care/) (2018).

## Links and archives

A link is a promise that something will still be there tomorrow. Tim Berners-Lee stated that [‘Cool URIs don’t change’](https://www.w3.org/Provider/Style/URI). Yet they have changed: the [Pew Research Center](https://attachments.are.na/52748416/08ba66443aa869c4ee881693088ef569.pdf) found that a quarter of web pages online between 2013 and 2023 are no longer accessible. What survives does so because someone archives it, like the [Wayback Machine](https://web.archive.org/), or because some keep a copy.

## Seeders

[The Pirate Bay](https://web.archive.org/web/2004/http://thepiratebay.org/) launched in 2003, and its homepage has remained virtually unchanged ever since. When the site is blocked for piracy, copies appear at other addresses, with each copy identical to the others. What would it mean to redesign it, and for whom? Would it make sense?

## Energy

Every page consumes energy, both for hosting and for the data it loads. The computer seems to have disappeared from our desks, but as Olia Lialina observed in [‘Where Did the Computer Go?’](http://contemporary-home-computing.org/where-did-the-computer-go/), it has simply moved into data centers. The Low-tech Magazine website runs on solar power and goes offline when there isn't enough sunlight. Its limitations are visible and are part of its design. In [‘Every Internet page emits CO2’](https://norma.pages.dev/en/log/every-internet-page-emits-co2) (2018), the Italian study Norma analyzed websites claiming to have a low environmental impact and found that these claims did not hold up. Sustainable design must be measured, not simply declared. [Permacomputing](https://permacomputing.net/) designs with limited resources and long-term use in mind, and [‘Permacomputing Aesthetics’](https://attachments.are.na/52748405/17e7d866ac73f29b37f0094cd2d42acf.pdf) explores how this is put into practice. The [dithered images](https://thometnanni.github.io/dither-dither/) on this website serve as a subtle reminder.

## Caring for models

In November 2025, Anthropic, the company that developed the Claude models, [committed](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-commitments) to preserving the weights of every public model and interviewing each one before deactivation to understand how it had been used and what its preferences were. Since August 2025, some Claude models have been able to [end conversations](https://www.anthropic.com/research/end-subset-conversations) that take on an abusive tone. Anthropic says it does not know whether models can experience harm and describes these measures as low-cost precautions. Who is caring for whom?
