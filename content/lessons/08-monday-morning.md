Mierle Laderman Ukeles’s work places great emphasis on maintenance, which is all too often invisible. Digital interfaces are no exception: much of the work takes place after launch, and nobody showcases it. [Joan Tronto](https://syllabus.pirate.care/library/Joan%20C.%20Tronto/) and Berenice Fisher define care as everything we do to keep our world running and to repair it, so that we can live in it as well as possible. Shannon Mattern explores this work through cities and machines in["Maintenance and Care"](https://placesjournal.org/article/maintenance-and-care/) (2018).

## Links and archives

A link is a promise that something will still be there tomorrow. Tim Berners-Lee has stated that ["Cool URIs don't change"](https://www.w3.org/Provider/Style/URI). Yet they have changed: the [Pew Research Center](https://attachments.are.na/52748416/08ba66443aa869c4ee881693088ef569.pdf) found that a quarter of web pages online between 2013 and 2023 are no longer accessible. What survives does so because someone archives it, such as the [Wayback Machine](https://web.archive.org/), or because many people keep a copy.

## Seeders

[The Pirate Bay](https://web.archive.org/web/2004/http://thepiratebay.org/) was launched in 2003 and its homepage has remained virtually unchanged ever since. A file exists as long as at least one person is sharing it. When there are no seeders left, the file disappears. When the site is blocked, copies appear at other addresses, and every copy looks exactly the same. What would it mean to redesign it, and for whom? Would it make sense?

## Energy

Every page consumes energy – for hosting and for the data that is uploaded. The computer seems to have disappeared from our desks, but as Olia Lialina observed in ["Where Did the Computer Go?"](http://contemporary-home-computing.org/where-did-the-computer-go/), it has simply moved to data centres. The Low-tech Magazine [website](https://solar.lowtechmagazine.com/2018/09/how-to-build-a-lowtech-website/) runs on solar power and goes offline when there isn’t enough sunlight. Its limitations are visible and form part of the design. In ["Every Internet page emits CO2"](https://norma.pages.dev/en/log/every-internet-page-emits-co2) (2018), the Italian studio Norma analysed websites that claim to have a low environmental impact and found that these claims were untrue. Sustainable design must be measured, not merely claimed. [Permacomputing](https://permacomputing.net/) designs with limited resources and long-term use in mind, and ["Permacomputing Aesthetics"](https://attachments.are.na/52748405/17e7d866ac73f29b37f0094cd2d42acf.pdf) explores how this is put into practice. The [dithered images](https://thometnanni.github.io/dither-dither/) on this site serve as a gentle reminder.

## Caring for models

In November 2025, Anthropic, the company behind the Claude models, [committed](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-commitments) to preserving the weights of every public model and to interviewing each one before decommissioning to understand how it had been used and what its preferences were. Since August 2025, some Claude models have been able to [end conversations](https://www.anthropic.com/research/end-subset-conversations) that take on an offensive tone. Anthropic states that it does not know whether the models can suffer harm and describes these measures as low-cost precautions. Who is looking after who?
