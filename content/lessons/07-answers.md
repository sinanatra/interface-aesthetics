# Answers
## Why make something that can be generated in seconds?

> The visual is too loaded to be of any use.
> Vinzenz Aubry, ["In Defense of No Image"](https://vinzenzaubry.com/text/in-defense-of-no-image), 2024

[![In the age of information overload, the ultimate luxury is meaning and context.](https://d2w9rnfcy7mm78.cloudfront.net/52827931/original_1be44e685918d987f239e8049c8a524c.webp?1794834466?bc=0 "From Laurel Schwulst, My Website Is a Shifting House, 2018")](https://laurelschwulst.com/e/my-website-is-a-shifting-house/)

In May 2024 Google started answering searches itself, with an AI Overview on top of the results. Asked how to keep cheese from sliding off a pizza, it suggested [adding glue to the sauce](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/05/31/1093019/why-are-googles-ai-overviews-results-so-bad/). The advice came from a [joke posted on Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/Pizza/comments/1a19s0/my_cheese_slides_off_the_pizza_too_easily/) in 2012. Google's answer was a joke by itself, but lost what made it a joke in the first place: meaning and context.


## No source to doubt

We used to search and get a list of results. Now we prompt and get one answer. Umberto Eco, in *The Infinity of Lists* (2009), treats the list as a way of knowing that never pretends to be complete. A page of results shows that something was chosen, and that there is more. An answer looks finished.

## Without images

Even images now start from words. With image generators, Vinzenz Aubry notes in ["In Defense of No Image"](https://vinzenzaubry.com/text/in-defense-of-no-image) (2024), the relation flips: the prompt, the description, points to the image, not the other way round. His title answers Hito Steyerl's ["In Defense of the Poor Image"](https://www.e-flux.com/journal/10/61362/in-defense-of-the-poor-image/) (2009), about compressed copies that travel faster than any original. For Aubry, an image by itself doesn't represent anything. A photograph pointed to something that stood in front of the camera. A generated image points nowhere; a sentence points to it. 
This is the death of representation. If an image takes a second, the question is no longer how to make it, but why.

## Someone who looked

When anything can be generated, what's left to make is a position: who, where, why. A page written in plain HTML shows it. As [_HTML Energy_](https://html.energy/) reminds us, anyone who wants to publish on the web can write HTML, and what you write by hand you can read, change and keep. Alt text is what a screen reader speaks in place of an image, and [*Alt Text as Poetry*](https://alt-text-as-poetry.net/) treats it as writing, not as a chore: words chosen by someone who looked.
