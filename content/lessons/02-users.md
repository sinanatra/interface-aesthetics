# Users
## Who is the user?

> There are only two industries which refer to their customers as users, drugs and computers.
> [Edward Tufte, 1997](https://cryptome.org/jya/tufte.htm)

A mug says "tradition" in many languages. The Arabic comes out in loose, disconnected letters, set by someone who couldn't read it. [_Nope, Not Arabic_](https://www.notarabic.com/) collects these. Software is built for an expected user, and if you are not that user, it doesn't work for you.

## Users or people

In 2006 Don Norman asked designers to [stop saying "users"](https://jnd.org/words-matter-talk-about-people-not-customers-not-consumers-not-users/): he'd rather call them people. Olia Lialina defends the word in ["Turing Complete User"](http://contemporary-home-computing.org/turing-complete-user/). Being a user is the last reminder that there is a computer, programmed by someone else. Call everyone people and the line between those who build and those who use disappears, and with it the rights users could claim. In ["Rich User Experience"](http://contemporary-home-computing.org/RUE/) she follows the renaming through design: computer becomes technology, interface becomes experience, until we forget there is a machine at all.
Italian mental health services call the people in their care _utenti_, users. In the asylums Franco Basaglia fought to close, they were _internati_.

## From the margins

The expected user is a choice. Sasha Costanza-Chock's [_Design Justice_](https://designjustice.mitpress.mit.edu/) asks for design led by the communities it affects. Ruha Benjamin, in [*Race After Technology*](https://mycours.es/ems2/files/2020/09/Ruha-Benjamin-%E2%80%93-Race-After-Technology-Introduction.pdf), shows how systems that look neutral reproduce racial discrimination. Hackers & Designers' [Feminist Search Tool](https://hackersanddesigners.nl/p/Feminist_Search_Engine) brings it down to a library search, which decides what gets found and what doesn't.
