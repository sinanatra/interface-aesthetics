# Users

> [Edward Tufte, 1997](https://cryptome.org/jya/tufte.htm)

On a mug, the word ‘tradition’ is written in several languages, but the Arabic text is incorrect. It was written by someone who couldn’t read it, using characters from an incompatible keyboard. [_Nope, Not Arabic_](https://www.notarabic.com/) collects examples like this.

## Users or people

In 2006, Don Norman asked designers to [stop saying ‘users’](https://jnd.org/words-matter-talk-about-people-not-customers-not-consumers-not-users/) and to use the term ‘people’ instead. Olia Lialina, on the other hand, defends the term in [‘Turing Complete User’](http://contemporary-home-computing.org/turing-complete-user/). Being a user is the final reminder of the existence of a computer, programmed by someone else. Changing this term removes that boundary between the creator and the user, and with it the rights that users might claim. In [‘Rich User Experience’](http://contemporary-home-computing.org/RUE/) the author analyses this process: the computer becomes technology, the interface becomes an experience, to the point of concealing the machine behind it.

## From the margins

The intended user is a choice. [_‘Design Justice’_](https://designjustice.mitpress.mit.edu/) by Sasha Costanza-Chock calls for design driven by the communities it affects. Ruha Benjamin, in [_‘Race After Technology’_](https://mycours.es/ems2/files/2020/09/Ruha-Benjamin-%E2%80%93-Race-After-Technology-Introduction.pdf), shows how systems that appear neutral reproduce racial discrimination. The
[“Feminist Search Engine”](https://hackersanddesigners.nl/p/Feminist_Search_Engine) by Hackers & Designers asks: “Who is taking responsibility for which part of the search process: us, the users, the researcher, the library, the algorithm? And how does this influence our search results?”
