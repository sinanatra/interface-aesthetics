# Doorknobs

## What does an interface make you do?

> We know how to open doors without knobs.
> Olia Lialina, “Turing Complete User”, 2012

![](https://studiomoniker.com/videos/direct/173770637.hd.mp4)

[![Studio Moniker, Puff Up Club](https://studiomoniker.com/thumbs/projects/puff-up-club/states/installation-1920x1272-q85.jpg "Studio Moniker, Puff Up Club")](https://studiomoniker.com/projects/puff-up-club)

A doorknob tells your hand where to go. A button does the same on a screen. Design calls this _affordance_, a word [Don Norman](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/301153.301168) borrowed from the psychologist James Gibson. Anatoly Zenkov [steals](https://anatolyzenkov.com/stolen-buttons) a button from every website he visits. Side by side, they look like one.

## One app per purpose

A door does one thing, a computer can become anything. In ["Once Again, The Doorknob"](https://contemporary-home-computing.org/affordance/) (2018) Olia Lialina watches design narrow the computer down to one app per purpose. You don't need a dating app, she says, you can date in Excel. Amelia Wattenberger notes that our interfaces have [lost their senses](https://wattenberger.com/thoughts/our-interfaces-have-lost-their-senses): we sculpt with our hands and scan with our eyes, then flatten all of it into tapping on glass.

## The cursor as material

On a screen every gesture is recorded. Vilém Flusser read gestures like writing or telephoning as expressions of freedom ([*Gestures*](https://www.upress.umn.edu/9780816691289/gestures/)); online they are counted, and can be turned back on us. Studio Moniker turns this around: thousands of strangers' cursors move together in [_Do Not Touch_](https://puckey.studio/projects/do-not-touch), and clicks inflate a [real balloon](https://studiomoniker.com/projects/puff-up-club) until it pops. [_Pointer Pointer_](https://pointerpointer.com/) answers your cursor with a photo of someone pointing right at it.

## Playing with the parts

The web is made of standard parts, and parts can be played with. Evan Roth's [_All HTML_](https://esoteric.codes/blog/all-html) puts every HTML tag on one page. Katherine Yang [jams](https://kayserifserif.place/work/every-element-is-an-html/) with the same elements. JODI made the [error page](http://404.jodi.org/) the work. Matthew Fuller, in [*Behind the Blip*](https://monoskop.org/log/?p=1108), calls this critical software: software that shows its own assumptions.

_In short:_ An interface tells you what to do but you don’t have to listen.
