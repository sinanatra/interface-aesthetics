Gardens

Can a website be inhabited?

What kind of room is a website? Or is a website more like a house? A boat? A cloud? A garden? A puddle?

Laurel Schwulst, ‘My Website Is a Shifting House’, 2018

A digital interface is usually planned with a launch date. A garden has no such date, and is never finished: it grows thanks to the care it receives and changes depending on who looks after it.
Derek Jarman planted his garden next to an unfenced nuclear power station. Since his death, others have continued to look after it. In 1995, the Telegarden literally merged a website with a garden: anyone online could plant and water seeds using a robotic arm. Laurel Schwulst asks her students to describe a website as a place where someone lives.

Homepages

Before social media, people built their own pages and the web resembled them. GeoCities grouped them into neighbourhoods; when it closed in 2009, the saved copy was represented as a map. This amateurish language is the ‘Vernacular Web’. Summer represents the volatility of these pages. The animation works until all these sites disappear or change.

Spreadsheets

Almost everyone knows how to read and fill in a spreadsheet. Rows and columns provide a structure; people fill them in, make notes, colour them and keep them organised. The structure is a simple text file that any programme can open (Guillaume Plique wrote a love letter to it). These grids can contain drawings, publications and indexes. A simple format, yet open to interpretation. My own site is a spreadsheet. The spreadsheet becomes data. Lev Manovich described the database and narrative as natural enemies. In the spreadsheet, they share the same cells.

Coming back

Some places exist solely because people keep returning to them. Webrings and the Corners of the Internet Database link small sites chosen by people, not by an algorithm. On r/place, a shared canvas became a territory: groups drew together and defended what they drew.

Language

As HTML Energy reminds us, anyone who wants to publish on the web can write in HTML, and what is written by hand can be easily read, edited and preserved. Alternative text is what a screen reader reads in place of an image, and Alt Text as Poetry treats it as writing, not as a chore: words chosen by someone who looked.


TLDR: A website can be tended and lived in, like a homepage or a shared spreadsheet.
Reading: Laurel Schwulst, ‘My Website Is a Shifting House’, 2018
