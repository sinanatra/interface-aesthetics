Users

Who is the user?

There are only two industries which refer to their customers as users, drugs and software.

Edward Tufte, 1997

On a mug, the word ‘tradition’ is written in several languages, but the Arabic text is incorrect. It was written by someone who couldn’t read it, using characters from an incompatible keyboard. Nope, Not Arabic collects examples like this.

Users or people

In 2006, Don Norman asked designers to stop saying ‘users’ and to use the term ‘people’ instead. Olia Lialina, on the other hand, defends the term in ‘Turing Complete User’. Being a user is the final reminder of the existence of a computer, programmed by someone else. Changing this term removes that boundary between the creator and the user, and with it the rights that users might claim. In ‘Rich User Experience’ the author analyses this process: the computer becomes technology, the interface becomes an experience, to the point of concealing the machine behind it.

From the margins

The intended user is a choice. Design Justice by Sasha Costanza-Chock calls for design driven by the communities it affects. Ruha Benjamin, in Race After Technology, shows how systems that appear neutral reproduce racial discrimination. The
‘Feminist Search Engine’ by Hackers & Designers asks: ‘Who is taking responsibility for which part of the search process: us, the users, the researcher, the library, the algorithm? And how does this influence our search results?’


TLDR: ‘User’ names a relation of power.
Reading: Olia Lialina, ‘Turing Complete User’, 2012
