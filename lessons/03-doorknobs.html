Doorknobs

What does an interface make you do?

We know how to open doors without knobs.

Olia Lialina, ‘Turing Complete User’, 2012

A doorknob tells the hand where to go. A button does the same on a screen. In ‘Once Again, The Doorknob’ (2018) Olia Lialina traces how affordance, a term Don Norman borrowed from the psychologist James Gibson, became the rule of interface design. Anatoly Zenkov steals a button from every website he visits. More or less,affordance makes them all look the same.

One app per purpose

A door does one thing, a computer can become anything. Lialina also observes how design reduces the computer to a single app per purpose (look at vibe coded websites). You don’t need a dating app, she says; you can even go on a date using Excel. Amelia Wattenberger notes that our interfaces have lost their senses: we sculpt with our hands and scrutinise with our eyes, only to flatten everything into a simple tap on the glass.

The cursor as material

Every gesture is recorded on a screen. Vilém Flusser interpreted gestures such as writing or making a phone call as expressions of freedom (Gestures); online, they are counted and can be used against us. Studio Moniker turns this perspective on its head: in Do Not Touch, thousands of cursors belonging to strangers move in unison, or they ask you for clicks inflate a real balloon until it pops. Pointer Pointer responds to your cursor with a photo of someone pointing at that exact spot.
In 1994 ART+COM built a virtual globe, allowing users to explore the digital world by spinning a physical globe.

Playing with the parts

The web is made up of standard components, and you can play with these components. Evan Roth’s All HTML brings together all HTML tags on a single page. Katherine Yang improvises with the same elements. JODI trasnformed the error page into the artwork, in Scrollbar Composition, the piece is the scrollbar. Matthew Fuller, in Behind the Blip, defines this approach as ‘critical software’: software that reveals its own assumptions.


TLDR: An interface tells you what to do but you don’t have to listen.
Reading: Olia Lialina, ‘Once Again, The Doorknob’, 2018
